# Design
## Direction
The replacement direction is a tactile, quietly playful museum room for potential collaborators and clients. The current homepage remains in place; `/room/` is a local design prototype for reviewing the replacement before deployment. The room replaces the previous editorial visual concept.
## System
Warm charcoal, ivory, walnut, Persian rust red, and subdued portal blue. Self-hosted Manrope. Visible name and Wintor role above a fixed, elevated 3D room. Objects can sit at natural angles; the portal faces the visitor. No physical lamps, plaques, tags, or banners. Object lighting plus soft ambient fill preserves silhouette clarity.
## Interaction
Undiscovered objects use natural resting states instead of a shared dark material mask. The portal is a wavering closed seam; the first click opens it, and subsequent clicks visit Wintor in a new tab. Server, laptop, and desktop are powered off until selected, then play startup sequences and settle to idle; repeated clicks restart them. Concise tags appear on hover after discovery: headset VR, phone/tablet AR app, duck duck; no tag on server or plants. Tool tokens retain their names. Exposed project icons show project names instead of inheriting Iran from the carpet. The oval Portal-inspired opening reveals an existing place photograph with aspect-preserving centered cover cropping. The carpet has woven top and underside materials, bound thickness, and moving fringe. The entire carpet can be grabbed and dragged around the floor. A damped cloth grid gives local lifting, fabric lag, room bounds and settling. Fixed floor tiles underneath reveal GGJG, Glass Garden, Northern Farm and Tactics as the carpet moves. Directional buttons and a lay-flat reset mirror dragging. GGJG is former involvement, not a current affiliation. Laptop stickers represent verified tools; the notebook remains a page-turn prop while direct contact stays separate. Server sits in the back-left corner. The left-wall desk has a monitor, keyboard, mouse, tower and laptop, with a racing-style gaming chair. Beneath the Shosho and Wintor logo lights, a modern open-base cantilever toolbox has two rising trays, dividers, connecting arms, a ribbed lid, protective corners and metal latches. Opening shakes it and ejects every stored tool. Eleven tool objects represent Svelte, Azure, Unity, Codex, Python, Plesk, C#, Git, GitHub Actions, Vite and npm. Icons mix cubes, coins and badges; they can be picked up, dropped onto the floor or returned to highlighted trays. Put-in / take-out controls mirror dragging. Stored icons move with folding trays. Reopening ejects stored contents again while objects already on the floor stay put. The notebook turns its page and navigates to the contact section, focusing its heading. A direct contact link remains available. Official Shosho and Wintor silhouettes glow as coral and cyan neon wall decorations and link to their websites; these current affiliations remain visible at entry. HTML story controls provide keyboard and touch alternatives; reduced motion suppresses ambient animation. The official undated GGJG logo appears under the carpet. A programming duck squashes and makes a synthesized quack on click, with a mute control and no autoplay audio. A stand-mounted binocular VR headset with lenses, cushion and curved straps briefly displaces the whole room with a bounded RGB glitch. Phone and tablet stay on their cabinet and toggle their screens on or off on click. A small plant and the duck share a left-wall stand beneath an abstract print; a tall plant stays on the floor. Plants sway softly; foliage clicks yellow and drop one random live leaf, while pot clicks regrow fallen leaves. Keyboard/touch controls mirror all surprises. Reduced motion suppresses ambient motion and replaces the moving glitch with a still tint/displacement. No submission backend or deployment is selected.
